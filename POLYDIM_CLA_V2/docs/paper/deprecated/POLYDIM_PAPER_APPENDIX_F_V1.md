## Appendix F — The ALIGN Protocol: Alignment Strategies for Heterogeneous Latent Spaces

Section 5.2 of the paper presents the ALIGN protocol at the level of Definition 5.2 and
Proposition 5.2. This appendix provides the three concrete alignment strategies, their
mathematical properties, and a selection guide.

### F.1 The Alignment Problem

Let AI_A operate in R^{d_A} and AI_B operate in R^{d_B}, where d_A ≠ d_B in general.
(Example: GPT-4 d_A = 12,288; Claude d_B = 8,192; Gemini d_C = 4,096.)

A transformation T_A generated by AI_A is a matrix (or LoRA pair) designed to operate on
vectors in R^{d_A}. AI_B cannot directly apply T_A to its state in R^{d_B}. The ALIGN
protocol computes a mapping M : R^{d_A} → R^{d_B} (or a shared projection to R^r) such
that T_A can be transported to AI_B's space with preserved algebraic structure.

**Formal requirement (from Proposition 5.2):** The transported transformation T_B must satisfy:

    COMPOSE(T_B^(1), T_B^(2)) = M · COMPOSE(T_A^(1), T_A^(2)) · M†

where M† is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of M. That is, transporting and then composing
must be equivalent to composing and then transporting.

### F.2 Strategy 1 — Orthogonal Procrustes Alignment

**When to use:** When there exists a shared codebook C of anchor pairs — objects for which
both AI_A and AI_B have known local representations. This is the most common case when
the two AIs have overlapping training data.

**Mathematical setup:**

Let {(a_i, b_i)} be K anchor pairs, where a_i ∈ R^{d_A} and b_i ∈ R^{d_B} are the
representations of the same semantic object in each system's latent space. If d_A = d_B,
the Orthogonal Procrustes problem [Schönemann, 1966] finds:

    M* = argmin_{R: R^T R = I} ‖R · A_anchors - B_anchors‖_F²

Solution via SVD: let A^T B = U Σ V^T, then M* = U · V^T.

If d_A ≠ d_B, the generalized Procrustes problem projects both spaces to a common dimension
r = min(d_A, d_B) first, then aligns. In POLYDIM, r = RANK_R from the `.polydim` header,
ensuring the alignment operates in the same rank-reduced space used by the LoRA transforms.

**Properties:**
- Preserves inner products: ⟨M·u, M·v⟩ = ⟨u, v⟩ (for orthogonal M)
- Preserves cosine similarity between vectors
- Preserves the quasi-orthogonality property required by MIX (see Appendix G)
- Computable in O(K·min(d_A, d_B)²) time via SVD

**Limitation:** Requires K ≥ min(d_A, d_B) anchor pairs for a uniquely determined solution.
For large dimension mismatches, the required number of anchors may be impractical.

### F.3 Strategy 2 — Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA)

**When to use:** When anchor pairs are unavailable but there exist parallel corpora — sets
of semantically equivalent inputs that both systems have processed. This is the zero-shot
alignment case: no explicit correspondences, but distributional similarity.

**Mathematical setup:**

Let X_A ∈ R^{n×d_A} and X_B ∈ R^{n×d_B} be activation matrices from n parallel inputs
(same semantic content processed by each AI independently). CCA finds projections W_A ∈ R^{d_A×r}
and W_B ∈ R^{d_B×r} that maximize the correlation between the projected representations:

    (W_A*, W_B*) = argmax_{W_A, W_B} corr(X_A · W_A, X_B · W_B)

Solution: via generalized eigenvalue problem on the cross-covariance matrix Σ_{AB}.

**Properties:**
- Does not require explicit anchor pairs
- Finds the r-dimensional subspace of maximum semantic agreement between the two systems
- The projections W_A, W_B serve as the alignment matrices M_A and M_B

**For POLYDIM:** The shared codebook C of GEO_IDs is exactly the set of anchor pairs. When
a GEO_ID codebook exists (which is a design goal of POLYDIM), Procrustes is preferred.
CCA is the fallback for first contact between two AI systems with no prior GEO_ID exchange.

### F.4 Strategy 3 — VSA Shared Codebook (Hyperdimensional Alignment)

**When to use:** When a universal shared codebook H ⊂ R^D is available — a pre-defined
set of hypervectors in a high-dimensional space D ≫ max(d_A, d_B) that serves as a
"Rosetta Stone" for all participating AI systems.

**Mathematical setup:**

Let H = {h_1, ..., h_K} ⊂ R^D be the shared hypervector codebook (D ≥ 10,000, K = number
of semantic primitives). Each AI system learns a projection matrix P_X : R^{d_X} → R^D
such that local representations project to their corresponding codebook vectors with high
cosine similarity:

    cos(P_X(local_repr(concept_i)), h_i) ≈ 1  for all i

**Communication via shared codebook:**

AI_A with state s_A ∈ R^{d_A} transmits transformation T_A as:
1. Project T_A to H: T_H = P_A(T_A) ∈ R^D (via composition of the projection with T_A)
2. AI_B receives T_H and reverses: T_B = P_B^†(T_H) ∈ R^{d_B}

The shared hypervector space H is the POLYDIM universal subspace — the geometric implementation
of the GEO_ID homotopy class. Two AI systems agree that a vector belongs to the same
homotopy class if and only if their projections to H have high cosine similarity.

**Properties:**
- Does not require direct geometric knowledge of the partner's latent space
- Scales to N participating AIs without O(N²) pairwise alignments
- The POLYDIM GEO_ID codebook is exactly the shared H (when fully realized)
- Requires D ≥ K for non-trivially quasi-orthogonal codebook vectors (by JL Lemma; see App. G)

**Limitation:** Requires pre-agreement on H. For two AI systems encountering each other for
the first time with no shared codebook, this strategy cannot bootstrap from nothing.
It assumes a POLYDIM consortium that maintains and distributes H.

### F.5 Strategy Selection Guide

| Condition | Recommended Strategy |
|---|---|
| Shared GEO_ID codebook available | Strategy 3 (VSA) — most scalable |
| Anchor pairs available (K ≥ min(d_A,d_B)) | Strategy 1 (Procrustes) — optimal |
| Parallel corpora available, no anchors | Strategy 2 (CCA) — zero-shot |
| None of the above | Exchange MODO_S descriptions; build codebook first |

In practice, the POLYDIM bootstrap V0.3 implements a simplified Strategy 1 using the
9 native subspace names as anchors — the string aliases DIM_SQL, DIM_FLUTTER, etc. serve
as the codebook C, with their activation vectors serving as the anchor pairs. This
achieves the align_score = 0.9993 reported in Section 8.2.

The production ALIGN protocol (TASK pending) will implement Strategy 3 using the full
GEO_ID codebook as the shared hypervector space.

### F.6 Zero-Serialization Theorem (Extended)

The zero-serialization property holds under all three strategies, subject to the
alignment quality of M:

**Theorem F.1 (Zero-Serialization, Extended).** Let M be the alignment matrix computed
by any of the three strategies above, with alignment quality q = corr(M·A_anchors, B_anchors).
Then the semantic fidelity of the POLYDIM communication protocol is:

    Fidelity(T_B) = q · Fidelity(T_A)

where Fidelity measures cosine similarity between intended and received semantic content.

For Strategy 1 (Procrustes) with d_A = d_B and K sufficient anchors: q = 1.0 (perfect alignment).
For Strategy 2 (CCA): q = λ_max(Σ_{AB}) / √(λ_max(Σ_{AA}) · λ_max(Σ_{BB})) < 1.0.
For Strategy 3 (VSA): q depends on the quality of the projection matrices P_A, P_B.

The bootstrap's align_score of 0.9993 corresponds to Strategy 1 on the 9-subspace codebook,
confirming near-perfect alignment quality for the current subspace vocabulary.
